📜 HISTORICAL ARCHIVE

🌋 FOUR ERUPTION STYLES

HAWAIIAN
Generally associated with highly fluid lava and relatively gentle, steady flows.
STROMBOLIAN
Known for intermittent bursts caused by gas bubbles breaking through magma.
VULCANIAN
Short explosive eruptions can produce ash-rich clouds when viscous magma or a plug is disrupted.
PLINIAN
Powerful sustained eruption columns can rise many kilometres into the atmosphere.

🚀 THE V-2 MISSILE

DEVELOPMENT
Developed in Germany in the late 1930s and early 1940s by a team led by Wernher von Braun at Peenemünde.
FIRST SUCCESSFUL LAUNCH — 1942
The first successful test launch took place on 3 October 1942. The V-2 is widely regarded as the world's first long-range guided ballistic missile.
HOW IT WORKED
A liquid-fuel engine burning ethanol with liquid oxygen, steered by gyroscopes and control surfaces. It stood about 14 metres tall and had a range of roughly 320 km.
REACHING SPACE — 1944
A V-2 test flight in June 1944 is regarded as the first human-made object to reach space.
WARTIME USE
Used operationally from September 1944. It caused great civilian suffering, and its production relied on forced labour — an important part of its history.
LEGACY
After the war, captured V-2s and engineers influenced the early rocket programmes of the United States and the Soviet Union.

🔥 EARLY ROCKETRY: MYSOREAN ROCKETS

18TH CENTURY
Mysorean forces developed and used iron-cased war rockets associated with the armies of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan.
ANGLO-MYSORE WARS
British forces encountered Mysorean rockets during conflicts with Mysore.
CONGREVE ROCKETS
British officers studied Mysorean rocket technology. William Congreve later developed a British military rocket system.
HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Mysorean rockets form an important chapter in early military rocketry — long before the guided, liquid-fuel missiles of the 20th century.
